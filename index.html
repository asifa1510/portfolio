Physics-Informed Hidden Regime Discovery and Local Symbolic Law Learning for Semi-Batch Reactors

Asifa S

National University of Singapore — IRIS Summer Research, 2026

Advisor: Assoc. Prof. Lakshminarayanan Samavedham

Developed a physics-informed machine learning framework for discovering hidden operating regimes in the nonlinear Chylla–Haase semi-batch polymerization reactor. Rather than fitting a single global equation, the framework learns temporal representations and discovers distinct local operating behaviours.

  • Constructed a physics-consistent dataset comprising 396,220 observations across 220 reactor trajectories.
  • Implemented a Mamba-based selective state-space architecture integrating physics-informed input features.
  • Applied Gaussian Mixture Models to learned temporal embeddings to discover eight unlabelled operating regimes.
  • Used regime-conditioned symbolic regression to derive interpretable local equations describing temperature-change dynamics.
  • Evaluated generalization on validation, test and out-of-distribution trajectories.
Results: 8 discovered operating regimes · 43.4% lower test RMSE · 17.6% lower out-of-distribution RMSE compared with the global symbolic-law baseline.

Mamba · State-Space Models · Physics-Informed ML · Gaussian Mixture Models · Symbolic Regression · Python · PyTorch

▣ Research experience ▣ Framework ▣ Results
Abstract

Nonlinear reactor behaviour changes across operating conditions involving reaction heat, feed rate, viscosity and cooling dynamics. This work investigates whether temporal representation learning can uncover these operating behaviours without manual regime annotations. Physics-informed features, selective state-space modelling and probabilistic clustering are combined with symbolic regression to obtain local, interpretable dynamical laws.

Surgical Error Prediction in Robotic-Assisted Cholecystectomy

Asifa S

Ongoing Research · 2026

Investigating uncertainty-aware spatiotemporal learning for anticipating adverse surgical events from operative video sequences. The research combines surgical scene understanding, anatomical segmentation, instrument motion analysis and temporal representations.

  • Surgical phase recognition and temporal modelling of procedural dynamics.
  • Optical-flow-based motion analysis and spatiotemporal feature extraction.
